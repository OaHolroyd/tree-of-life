Animalia

Animals

Animals are multicellular, eukaryotic organisms belonging to the biological kingdom Animalia. With few exceptions, animals consume organic material, breathe oxygen, have myocytes and are able to move, can reproduce sexually, and grow from a hollow sphere of cells, the blastula, during embryonic development. Animals form a clade, meaning that they arose from a single common ancestor. Over 1.5 million living animal species have been described, of which around 1.05 million are insects, over 85,000 are molluscs, and around 65,000 are vertebrates.

Information & FAQs

How do I play?

Type an animal into the search input box. If you guess the hidden species, you win! If your guess is not correct then it will be added to the tree of life, along with the least inclusive clade containing your guess and the answer (or a previous guess if this one is further away).

What is a Clade?

A clade is a biological grouping that is composed of a common ancestor and all of its descendants. For example, Dinosauria is defined by the most recent common ancestor of Triceratops and modern birds. This does result in a divergence between common usage of some terms and their cladistic meaning; by this definition, humans are all fish, since we can trace our ancestry back to fish that emerged onto land 375 million years ago in the Devonian.

What species are included?

I have tried to include a wide range of species to capture the diversity of animal life. The game has three levels of difficulty representing three differently sized lists of possible animals: the smallest list might contain "Elephant" as an answer, and the largest would contain the three separate species of extant elephant.

Bugs are so hard! Can I play without them?

Yes! As well as changing the diversity of animals that are possible answers/guesses you can change the root clade to one of your choosing.

What on earth is a lancelet!?

As well as some more commonly known species like lions or orcas, I have tried to include some less well-known species that showcase the evolutionary history of animal life. The lancelet is a chordate without a backbone - it just has a bundle of nerves and a flexible notochord rather than a spine like us.

Why is this game so Euro-centric?

The short answer is because that's where I come from! I mainly made this game because I wanted to play a version of it with a wider variety of animals than the existing Metazooa (from which this is very obviously derived!). I essentially added animals that I'd heard of, and so if I've not included your favourite lizard from your home country I'm very sorry!

How are the links between clades defined?

The tree that underpins the game is derived from Wikispecies, which defines almost a million(!) clades, from which I have extracted a subset. This database is freely available and can be edited by anyone. That being said, there are still many groups where there is uncertainty in their exact evolutionary origins, such as bats, comb jellies, and sponges. In these cases I have manually adjusted the tree to account for recent research and to break up polytomies to improve gameplay.
More detail on exactly how the underlying data is cleaned and cross-linked with information from Wikipedia can be found at the game's GitHub repository.

I've spotted an error!

If you spot an error, whether it is a typo, incorrect clade linkage, or bad image, feel free to raise it as an issue on the project's GitHub repository so that I can fix it and improve the game for everyone.
